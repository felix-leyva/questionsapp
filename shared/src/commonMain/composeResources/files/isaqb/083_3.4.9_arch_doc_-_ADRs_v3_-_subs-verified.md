## Question 1

What is the primary purpose of "Architectural Decision Records" (ADRs)?

- [ ] To replace all other forms of architecture documentation.
- [x] To document, communicate, and learn from architectural decisions, promoting consistency and
  risk management.
- [ ] To automatically generate code from architectural decisions.
- [ ] To track individual developer performance.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** ADRs document, communicate, and enable learning from architectural decisions,
promoting consistency and risk management. Documentation replacement, code generation, and
performance tracking are separate concerns outside ADR purposes.

</p>
</details>

## Question 2

Which of the following are typical components of an ADR's structure?

- [x] Title, Context, Decision, Status, Consequences, Author and Date.
- [ ] Project budget, team members, and daily tasks.
- [ ] Source code snippets and test results.
- [ ] User interface mockups and wireframes.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** ADR structure includes Title, Context, Decision, Status, Consequences, Author/Date.
Budget/team/tasks, code/tests, and UI mockups are project artifacts, not ADR structural components.

</p>
</details>

## Question 3

What is the purpose of the "Context" section in an ADR?

- [ ] To provide a brief label for the decision.
- [x] To describe factors such as technical, organizational, and social influences that led to the
  decision.
- [ ] To state the final decision concisely.
- [ ] To list the expected positive or negative impacts.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Context describes technical, organizational, and social influences leading to
decisions. Brief labels (Title), final decisions (Decision section), and impacts (Consequences
section) are separate ADR components.

</p>
</details>

## Question 4

What does the "Status" field in an ADR indicate?

- [ ] The current progress of the implementation.
- [x] Whether the ADR is proposed, accepted, or deprecated (if replaced by a newer version).
- [ ] The level of urgency of the decision.
- [ ] The number of people who agree with the decision.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Status indicates ADR lifecycle state: proposed, accepted, or deprecated (replaced).
Implementation progress, urgency level, and agreement count are project tracking metrics, not ADR
status.

</p>
</details>

## Question 5

How do ADRs help in understanding the evolution of architecture over time?

- [ ] They automatically update the architecture diagrams.
- [x] They help understand why architecture evolved in a specific way, even after a long time, by
  providing context and justification for decisions.
- [ ] They eliminate the need for any historical records.
- [ ] They only document the final state of the architecture.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** ADRs enable understanding of architectural evolution through preserved context and
justification for re-evaluation. Automatic diagram updates, historical record elimination, and
final-state-only documentation all miss ADRs' evolutionary documentation purpose.

</p>
</details>
