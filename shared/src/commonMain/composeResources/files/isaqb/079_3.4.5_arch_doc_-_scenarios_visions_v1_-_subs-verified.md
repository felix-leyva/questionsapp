## Question 1

What is the primary purpose of "Scenarios and Visions" in software architecture documentation?

- [ ] To provide a detailed technical specification of the system.
- [x] To tell exemplary stories from the business domain in a narrative form, describing typical or
  interesting episodes.
- [ ] To define the programming language to be used.
- [ ] To manage project timelines and resources.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Scenarios/visions tell business domain stories in narrative form, describing
typical/interesting episodes. Technical specifications, language definitions, and project management
are separate concerns outside narrative domain storytelling.

</p>
</details>

## Question 2

How do scenarios and visions facilitate communication between project participants?

- [ ] By eliminating the need for any verbal communication.
- [x] By creating precedent cases that people can discuss and clarify with each other, making
  unclear aspects and unknown terms easier to identify.
- [ ] By providing a rigid set of rules for interaction.
- [ ] By automating the entire communication process.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Scenarios/visions create discussable precedent cases, facilitating clarification of
unclear aspects and unknown terms. Communication elimination, rigid rules, and automation all
contradict scenarios' collaborative discussion-enabling purpose.

</p>
</details>

## Question 3

What is the difference between scenarios that depict the "current situation" and those that describe
a "desired situation"?

- [ ] Scenarios of the current situation are always more detailed.
- [x] Scenarios of the current situation describe the work context and business processes, while
  those of a desired situation (visions) illustrate how the desired system is to be used.
- [ ] Scenarios of the current situation are only for developers, while visions are for users.
- [ ] There is no difference; the terms are interchangeable.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Current-situation scenarios describe existing work context/processes; visions
illustrate desired system usage in known/imaginary contexts. Detail level, stakeholder restrictions,
and interchangeability all miss the current vs. desired temporal distinction.

</p>
</details>

## Question 4

What is an "open design scenario" and what is its purpose?

- [ ] A scenario that is left incomplete for future development.
- [x] A type of vision where an imaginary context is used to unleash creativity and lead to entirely
  new solutions.
- [ ] A scenario that is open to interpretation by different stakeholders.
- [ ] A scenario that describes a system with no predefined context.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Open design scenarios use imaginary contexts to unleash creativity for new solutions.
Incomplete scenarios, interpretative openness, and context absence all miss the intentional
creativity-unleashing through imaginary context exploration.

</p>
</details>

## Question 5

How do scenarios contribute to modern collaborative modeling methods like domain storytelling?

- [ ] They replace the need for such methods.
- [x] They form the foundation for gaining a deep understanding of the domain and developing
  tailored software.
- [ ] They are only used for formal documentation.
- [ ] They are irrelevant for collaborative modeling.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Scenarios form the foundation for domain understanding and tailored software
development in collaborative methods like domain storytelling. Replacement, formal-only use, and
irrelevance all miss scenarios' foundational role in collaborative modeling.

</p>
</details>
