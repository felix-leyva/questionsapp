## Question 1

What is the purpose of the framework introduced in the text regarding client-server cuts?

- [ ] To mandate thin client architecture for all system implementations.
- [ ] To establish rigid immutable rules for client-server protocols.
- [x] To help pick client-server cuts based on user interaction needs.
- [ ] To eliminate user involvement from system design decisions.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** The framework guides architects in selecting appropriate client-server cuts based on user interaction patterns and needs. It doesn't mandate architectures, establish rigid rules, or exclude users from design.

</p>
</details>

## Question 3

How does the user profile of a self-service machine (like an ATM) differ from that of a campaign manager, according to the text?

- [ ] ATMs require high IT knowledge with extensive task variety.
- [x] ATMs need zero IT knowledge, low variety, high support, low flexibility.
- [ ] ATMs are architected for high flexibility with minimal support.
- [ ] ATMs share identical profiles with campaign managers entirely.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** ATMs target users with minimal IT knowledge, performing limited tasks with high support and low flexibility (e.g., 95-year-old users). Campaign managers need high IT knowledge, task variety, and flexibility—opposite profiles.

</p>
</details>

## Question 5

## Question 7

How does the "Extent of support" dimension relate to the "IT knowledge" dimension for a user?

- [ ] They correlate directly: high IT knowledge demands high support.
- [x] They are inversely proportional; low IT knowledge often requires high support.
- [ ] They remain completely independent and unrelated.
- [ ] High IT knowledge universally eliminates all support requirements.
