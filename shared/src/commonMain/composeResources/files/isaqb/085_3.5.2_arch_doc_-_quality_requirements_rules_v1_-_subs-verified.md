## Question 1

What are the two main categories of quality requirements for good architecture documentation?

- [ ] High-level and low-level.
- [x] Functional and non-functional.
- [ ] Technical and business.
- [ ] Internal and external.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Documentation quality requirements are functional (answering architecture/technical
questions) and non-functional (up-to-date, comprehensible, maintainable). High/low-level,
technical/business, and internal/external are different categorization schemes.

</p>
</details>

## Question 2

Which of the following are examples of non-functional quality requirements for architecture
documentation?

- [x] Up-to-date and correct.
- [x] Suitable for its target group.
- [x] Clear and comprehensible.
- [x] Brief and compact.
- [x] Efficient to read and write.
- [x] Maintainable and cost-efficient.
- [ ] Answering concrete questions about architecture.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Non-functional requirements include up-to-date, correct, target-suitable, clear,
brief, efficient, maintainable, cost-efficient. Answering architectural questions is a functional
requirement, not a non-functional quality attribute.

</p>
</details>

## Question 3

What is the advice regarding sections of standard documentation structures that are left blank?

- [ ] They should be deleted to keep the document concise.
- [x] They should be marked with "not applicable" to avoid oversight.
- [ ] They should be filled with placeholder text.
- [ ] They indicate that the documentation is incomplete.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Blank sections should be marked "not applicable" to avoid oversight (intentional
omission vs. forgotten section). Deletion, placeholders, and incompleteness assumptions all miss the
explicit acknowledgment purpose.

</p>
</details>

## Question 4

What is the recommended perspective to adopt when writing architecture documentation?

- [ ] The architect's perspective.
- [ ] The project manager's perspective.
- [x] The reader's perspective.
- [ ] The developer's perspective.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Write from the reader's perspective (documentation is for them). Architect, project
manager, and developer perspectives all miss documentation's reader-serving purpose—clarity for
consumers, not producers.

</p>
</details>

## Question 5

What is the advice regarding the size and clarity of diagrams in architecture documentation?

- [ ] Diagrams should be as large as possible to show all details.
- [ ] Diagrams should only contain text, no visual elements.
- [x] Use small diagrams focusing on 5 to 9 elements to make them clearer and easier to understand.
- [ ] Diagrams should be generated automatically without any manual review.

<details>
<summary>Rationale</summary>
<p>

**Rationale:** Small diagrams with 5-9 elements maximize clarity and comprehension (cognitive load
management). Large diagrams, text-only, and unreviewed automation all contradict effective visual
communication principles.

</p>
</details>
